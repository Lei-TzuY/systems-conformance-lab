# Multipart form-data structural reduction

The multipart/form-data interoperability target has a domain-aware reducer for stable differential failures. The reducer operates on the same fixed, target-owned boundary used by the Python and Node adapters and only accepts the canonical CRLF framing emitted by the conformance corpus.

Reduction is intentionally fail-closed. Inputs with a preamble or epilogue, LF-only framing, malformed or non-ASCII header lines, a missing closing boundary, or ambiguous/non-canonical boundary framing are rejected rather than repaired. This prevents reduction from changing parser policy while searching for a smaller witness.

For canonical inputs the reducer can remove complete parts and shrink part bodies. Header reduction is limited to the two target-owned fully percent-encoded forms emitted by the filename-star mutators: a terminal `filename*=UTF-8''...` parameter and the deterministic non-empty-language form `filename*=UTF-8'en'...`. Their `%HH` payloads may be shortened only by whole encoded octets while charset and language metadata remain byte-for-byte intact. Other language tags, charsets, attr-char payloads, trailing parameters, and other Content-Disposition or Content-Type forms are never normalized or rewritten. Every emitted candidate is deterministic, unique, and strictly smaller in encoded bytes.

The integration tests validate the reducer against the real Python stdlib multipart path and Node `Response.formData()` target. A padded `charset=iso-8859-1` witness is discovered as a product mismatch, reduced while preserving its stable failure signature, written as a repro bundle, and replayed through the normal differential harness. Real-target filename-star validations cover both the empty-language and fixed `en` forms and prove that reduction can shrink each fully percent-encoded filename payload down to a one-octet witness while retaining the original RFC 5987 policy metadata, failure signature, and replayability.
